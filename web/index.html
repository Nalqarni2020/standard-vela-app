Regime

The written rule

Applied exactly, every load
  1. Regime. S&P (SPY) above a rising 8-day and a rising 20-day simple average, 8-day above 20-day. Rising means higher than three sessions ago. Red is below the 20-day with the 8 under the 20 and the 20 falling. Everything else is Yellow.
  2. Shelf. The latest swing low in the last 12 daily bars (lower than the two bars on each side), or a flush low (range at least 1.5 ATR, close in the bottom third, volume at least 1.5× average), whichever is newer. It is a base only after two closes above it and no close below it.
  3. Band. 1% to 2% above the shelf. Under 1% is still on the low. Over 2% is open air.
  4. Trigger. Daily: the confirmed close is above the 8-day, inside the band, on at least 1.3× the 20-day average volume. 5m and 15m: a VWAP reclaim within the last three bars, still above VWAP, inside the band, on at least 1.3× volume for that time of day.
  5. Stop and targets. Stop two cents under the shelf, never widened. Target 1 is the 20-day EMA. Target 2 is the 20-day SMA. Target 1 must pay 2 to 1 from the entry to the stop.
  6. Size. Core risk 2% of the account, speculative 1%, and the position is capped. Shares are rounded down.
  7. Clock. No new momentum entry from 11:00 to 14:00 New York. A daily swing is confirmed only by the cash close at or after 15:55. After-hours prints do not count.

Is the edge real?

Tests 144 settings on the list above: body ratio 0.40 to 0.80, each switch on and off. A real edge stays profitable on neighbouring settings. It then picks the best settings using only the first 60% of the period and tests them, untouched, on the last 40%.

How the test trades: long only, fully in or fully out. A signal on a bar's close is filled at the next bar's open, so the test never uses a price it could not have had. A sell signal closes the trade. With the stop switched on, the stop is the strong bullish low on the signal bar, it never moves, a gap through it fills at the open, and a buy whose next open is already at or under the stop is skipped. Costs are charged on both sides. Buy and hold covers the same bars. Past results do not predict future ones.

Edge lab

Published findings, tested on your data

Where profit actually comes from. Profit per trade = win rate × average win − loss rate × average loss. Most of that is set by the exit and the size, not the entry. This lab measures each piece on real prints so you can keep what works on your names and drop what does not.

  • Day trading is hard. In Taiwan, fewer than 1% of day traders were predictably profitable after costs (Barber, Lee, Liu and Odean, 2014). In Brazil, 97% of people who day-traded futures for more than 300 days lost money (Chague, De-Losso and Giovannetti, 2019).
  • Trends persist for months. Past 3 to 12 month winners kept outperforming (Jegadeesh and Titman, 1993; Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen, 2012), especially near a 52-week high (George and Hwang, 2004).
  • Very short moves tend to reverse. Last week's big losers bounced on average (Jegadeesh, 1990; Lehmann, 1990).
  • Unusual volume carries information. Stocks with a volume shock did better afterwards (Gervais, Kaniel and Mingelgrin, 2001).
  • Much of the stock return arrives overnight. Across US stocks, close-to-open returns were a large share of the total (Cooper, Cliff and Gulen, 2008; Lou, Polk and Skouras, 2019).
  • Intraday, two effects are documented. The first half hour predicted the last half hour of the S&P (Gao, Han, Li and Zhou, 2018). A 5-minute opening-range breakout on high relative volume stocks was profitable in a 2016 to 2023 test (Zarattini, Barbon and Aziz, 2024).
  • Size by risk, and less than you think. The Kelly fraction maximizes growth in theory, but estimates are noisy, so traders use a quarter or half of it. Scaling exposure down when volatility is high improved risk-adjusted returns (Moreira and Muir, 2017).

Published effects shrink after they are published and vary by period and stock. That is why each one is retested below rather than trusted.

1. Best exit for your entries

Takes every buy from your Candle break settings and filter, then exits the same entries five ways. Results are in R: 1R is the distance from entry to the starting stop.

2. Which published effects held on your list

Uses the list and period from Candle break. Each event is bought at the next open and sold at the close after the holding period, then compared with buying on any day.

3. Overnight or intraday?

Splits each name's return into close-to-open and open-to-close. If your names earn mostly overnight, a day trader who is flat every night misses most of the move.

4. Day-trade studies on this symbol

Opening-range breakout and last-half-hour momentum on this symbol's 5-minute bars, with costs. Robinhood serves a few weeks of 5-minute history, so treat the result as a first look, not proof.
